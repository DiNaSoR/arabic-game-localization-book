CHOOSING A LOCALIZATION TEAM

Who to hire, decided by what they have already shipped.

11 localizers in Muarrab's index have shipped Arabic inside a released game — published by the studio that made the game, not handed out as a patch afterwards. Those are the only teams recommended on this page, and each is placed against the kind of project its own record actually supports.

No studio pays to appear here. The order beneath every recommendation is the catalog's own count of official releases, and the one editorial judgement on the page — who leads each job class — is labelled as one.

How the leads were chosen. Arlation leads the flagship class on verified scale — 39 official releases across the largest publishers in the index. loclait leads the other two classes as the studio Muarrab would hand a mid-size release or a first Arabic build to. That second call is editorial; the counts beside it are not.

THREE KINDS OF JOB

Match the project, not the logo wall

A studio that is right for a flagship is usually wrong for a three-person team's first Arabic build, and the reverse is just as true. Each class below states what the work actually involves, names the team Muarrab would approach first, and lists the rest of the field in the order the record puts them.

WHAT GETS A TEAM ONTO THIS PAGE

Proven, not promised
One or more Arabic localizations shipped inside a released game and credited to the team by name in Muarrab's catalog. A studio with none is not recommended here, whatever its website says.
Official, not a patch
A community patch is a real achievement and the reason a lot of Arabic support exists, but it is not a commissioning reference. It does not count toward inclusion.
Documented, not asserted
Every figure comes from the catalog on this site, so the team can be checked entry by entry. Muarrab does not audit translation quality and does not rank on it.

JOB CLASS 01

AAA

A full-price flagship

A large game with a marketing date, a legal review, outsource vendors and a voice pipeline. The localization has to clear certification and cannot move the release date.

TYPICALLY INCLUDES
Full interface, subtitles and menus · Arabic voice recording and direction · publisher-side review and certification

3 teams in the index carry this scale of official work.

JOB CLASS 02

AA

A mid-size premium release

A funded title with a real audience but no dedicated localization department. Usually one language vendor, a fixed budget and a schedule that has already been agreed.

TYPICALLY INCLUDES
Interface, subtitles and menus · terminology and glossary ownership · QA passes and a patch cycle after launch

5 teams in the index carry this scale of official work.

JOB CLASS 03

Indie

A first Arabic build

A small team, a limited string count and no in-house localization experience. The risk is not tone — it is font coverage, bidirectional layout and a build that has never seen right-to-left text.

TYPICALLY INCLUDES
Text-only localization · font and shaping fixes · layout and mirrored-UI checks · a scope that fits a first pass

4 teams in the index carry this scale of official work.
